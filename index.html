Why this is the best 30 seconds of the appointment

Records aren't paperwork. They're the trophy at the finish line — and a moment the patient remembers.

Think about debond day. Someone has worked for months, sometimes years, and they're about to see their new smile for the first time. That photo is the celebration. When we nail it, the patient feels like a star, the case becomes something we can show off, and the work speaks for itself. When we rush it, all of that quietly slips away — and we don't get a do-over once they've walked out the door.

We don't just finish cases. We capture the exact moment someone falls in love with their smile.

Good news: we already do the hard part — the orthodontics. The photo is the easy, fun part. A few simple habits turn an "okay" snapshot into something genuinely beautiful. That's all this is.

04

The standards (a.k.a. what makes a photo chef's kiss)

The benchmark we shoot to — straight from the American Board of Orthodontics, with their own example photos.

📌
Quick context: the American Board of Orthodontics (ABO) publishes the formal photo standards below — the gold standard the whole field uses. The American Association of Orthodontists (AAO) doesn't publish its own photo recipe; its big message is simply that records should be complete and consistent. So we follow the ABO's technical bar and the AAO's spirit of thorough records.

Facial standards — the ABO's own ideal examples

Example facial photographs reproduced from the American Board of Orthodontics for training reference.

What the standard asks for

  • The views: profile (facing their right), frontal at rest, and frontal smiling — plus our signature profile smiling shot, four in all.
  • Head level & natural — on the Frankfort horizontal (ear-canal-to-under-eye line runs level), inter-pupillary line parallel to the frame.
  • Ears exposed so head position reads clearly; hair tucked back.
  • Eyes open, looking at the camera. Glasses off, no distracting jewelry.
  • Lighting that gives the face shape — soft and a touch directional, with a catchlight (sparkle) in the eyes.
  • Framed close & consistent — face and neck fill the frame the same way every time, so before/after line up.
🎨
Our setup: the ABO shows these on plain white. We get our best results at the east-corridor painted wall using Portrait › Studio Light › 2× — same rules for head, eyes, ears, and framing, with the cleanest light in the building. (Full recipe in Section 05.)

Spot-the-gremlins — learn to catch these

Even the ABO's "what-not-to-do" examples are great teachers. A quick glance and you'll start catching these in a heartbeat:

Easy fixes: step in closer, get them squared up and looking at the lens, tuck the hair, lose the jewelry, and let soft light do the work. (On our black backdrop, "shadow on the background" basically disappears — bonus.)

05

How we shoot a face (and make it look unreal)

After real-world testing, we landed on a simple recipe that makes faces look unreal — right here in our hallway. Settings, spot, smile.

① The recipe (our trial-winning setup) 🏆

We tried the options and this combo won. Memorize it:

Portrait Mode › Studio Light › 2× zoom
  • Open Camera → Portrait, then set the lighting effect to Studio Light.
  • Zoom to 2× — the most flattering, distortion-free framing.
  • Tap the face to focus, then lock it (tap-and-hold → AE/AF Lock) so nothing drifts between shots.
  • Grid on, lens wiped, Live Photos & filters off, max resolution.
  • 3–4 frames per view — keep the best. Free reps!
📸
Studio Light is doing the lighting for us — so there's no ring light needed, and none of that flat, dead-on ring-light look.

② Where we shoot — the painted wall 🎨

Facial photos go at the north wall of the east corridor — yep, the fun painted wall. We tested it: the light there is the best in the building, hands down better than the records room. Shoot the same spot every time and our before/afters line up beautifully.

  • Same spot, same distance, same height each time — make it muscle memory.
  • Let the hallway's great light carry it; Studio Light adds the polish.

③ Framing & the patient

  • Frontal: from just above the head down to the collarbones, face centered, eyes level, natural head position. Phone vertical.
  • Profile: patient's right side, leave a little space in front of the nose, chin level (Frankfort horizontal).
  • Glasses off, hair tucked behind the ears, trays out. Lip balm if lips are dry.
  • Same crop every time — patient centered, midline straight.
✓ Dial it in
  • 2× zoom — flattering, distortion-free.
  • Lock focus & exposure on the face.
  • Steady hands; 3–4 frames per view.
Watch-outs
  • The 0.5× ultra-wide — stretches the nose. Skip it.
  • Shooting from below (chin & nostrils) or above (shrinks the chin).
06

Getting the smile (the fun part)

This is where we kill two birds with one stone: a stunning photo and a moment they'll remember. "Say cheese" is out. Throwing a tiny party is in.

A real smile lives in the eyes — the corners crinkle, and the whole face lights up. You can't fake that, and you can't command it. So we don't ask for a smile; we give them a reason to. Talk to them, make them feel something good, and catch the real thing. Bonus: the patient leaves feeling celebrated, which is the whole point.

Don't ask for a smile. Hand them a moment and catch the one that shows up.

🎉 Make it a moment (especially at debond)

Hand them the mirror first. Let them see the new smile, hype them up, react with them — then lift the camera. The joy is already on their face; you're just there to catch it. That 20 seconds of celebration is often the highlight of their whole experience with us. Two birds, one beautiful stone. 🪨🐦🐦

✓ Quick posing wins
  • Shoulders square for frontals; true profile (facing right) for the lateral.
  • Chin level, hair off the ears, glasses off, trays out.
  • Reset the face between shots — fresh smile each time.
  • Keep chatting. Relaxed beats posed, every time.
💀 Expression killers
  • "Say cheeeese" — instant frozen, fake mouth.
  • Holding a smile too long — it dies in the eyes first.
  • Clenched teeth or pursed lips on the repose shot.
  • Squinting from harsh light pointed at their face.
  • Chin jutting forward or tucked into the chest.
07

Intraorals: today, we scan

Fast, clean, and comfy for the patient — and it mounts beautifully in Cloud9.

Our current method

  • Capture intraorals with the scanner — no retractors, no fuss, great detail.
  • Mount the scan in Cloud9 the same day it's taken. (More on that next section.)
  • At debond, also grab one center photo — a single frontal intraoral, teeth in occlusion, to capture the finished bite.
  • Trays out for scans and that center photo.

Clinical detail shots — let's sharpen these 🔍

Scans handle the routine records, but we still grab intraoral photos for the unique stuff — and this is honestly where we have the most room to grow. Let's get crisp at them:

  • Impacted or erupting teeth — show the position and the surrounding tissue clearly.
  • Partial brackets on rotated teeth — capture the bracket placement and the rotation we're correcting.
  • Anything noteworthy — anomalies, wear, recession, soft-tissue findings, breakage.

Make them count: dry the area, get close enough to fill the frame with the detail, tap to focus right on it, steady hands, soft even light. A little practice goes a long way — we'll drill these at the OSC.

Reference · if we bring manual intraoral photos back

Keeping this here so we're ready if we ever return to a full manual intraoral series. The standard set is five views: frontal, right & left buccal, and upper & lower occlusal (via mirror).

  1. Prep & dry the field.Clear, rinse, and dry the teeth — wet enamel and bubbles photograph as glare.
  2. Retract fully — two retractors.Pull clear so the retractors stay out of frame. Warm/air the mirror so it doesn't fog.
  3. Square to the occlusal plane.Frontal: midline centered, occlusal plane level and bisecting the frame. Laterals: show through the molars, gingiva visible.
  4. Frame close (≈1:1).Fill the frame with the dentition; tap to focus; soft even light to avoid hot spots.
  5. Reverse the mirror shots.Flip occlusal/mirror images so they read as a direct view of the patient before mounting.
  6. Quick check.No fingers, retractor edges, saliva, or blur — reshoot on the spot if needed.
08

Chair → Cloud9, same day every day

A photo or scan that isn't mounted yet is a photo that doesn't exist. Let's close the loop before we go home.

  1. Set up the same way every time.East-corridor painted wall, Portrait › Studio Light › 2×, lock focus & exposure on the face. Same spot makes it fast and keeps before/after lined up.
  2. Capture the facials, then the scan.Run the four facials in the same order, then intraorals on the scanner. At debond, add the one center intraoral photo and a fun bonus marketing smile.
  3. Grab the magic shot before they go.Glance at the frames while they're still here. Love it? Done. Want one better? It's 20 seconds while they're in the chair — easy.
  4. Mount everything in Cloud9 — end of day, same day.All facial photos and all scans land in the patient's Cloud9 cabinet by the end of the day they're taken. No backlog, ever.
  5. Flag the standouts for Autumn.Great finish? Send it Autumn's way — she curates what becomes socials, website, finish-day texts, and the photo book.

Mounting in Cloud9 — get left and right right ↔️

When scans and photos go in, double-check that the patient's right side is actually on the right. A flipped image is the classic records slip-up — and Cloud9 fixes it in one click. (It has nice clean-up tools too: rotate, crop, brightness/contrast — use them to keep the set sharp and consistent.)

🔒
Consent before the spotlight. Anything headed to social or the website needs a signed media release on file — and a parent/guardian's for minors. Internal records use is always fine.

Printable quick card

Print it, post it at the station. Everything that matters, on one card.

📷 Facial photo — fast checklist

  • Setting: Portrait › Studio Light › 2× zoom. Grid on, lens wiped, Live Photos & filters off.
  • Spot: east-corridor painted wall (best light) — same spot every time.
  • Lock it: tap the face, hold to lock focus & exposure. No ring light needed.
  • Prep: glasses off, hair behind ears, trays out, chin level.
  • Repose: "Lips rest gently, soft face." No clenching.
  • Smile: hand them a moment — don't say "cheese." Catch the real one.
  • Frames: 3–4 per view, keep the best. Reset between shots.
  • Views: profile rest · profile smile · frontal rest · frontal smile (+ optional ¾ oblique).

🦷 Intraoral — today

  • Scanner for intraorals → mount the scan in Cloud9 same day.
  • At debond: grab one center (frontal) photo, teeth in occlusion.
  • Detail photos for impacted teeth & partial brackets on rotations — close & sharp.
  • Check left/right when mounting — use Cloud9's mirror button if it's flipped.
  • Trays out for scans and photos.
  • Before bonding: full X-rays on file — pano at minimum.

🚪 Before they leave

  • Facials look great & scan captured? (Debond: center photo too?)
  • Standout finish flagged for Autumn (consent on file)?
  • Everything mounted in Cloud9 today — photos and scans?
